# Aggregation and Disaggregation

Use this method to move deliberately between the whole and its components.

## Aggregate first

Establish the overall magnitude, direction, denominator, time window, and baseline. Confirm that the aggregate is correctly calculated and meaningful.

## Disaggregate with a hypothesis

Break the whole across dimensions that could explain variation, such as:

- customer segment, cohort, geography, channel, product, workflow stage, or time;
- acquisition source, tenure, behavior, risk, cost, or value;
- organizational unit, technology component, or causal mechanism.

Do not slice indiscriminately. Every cut should test a hypothesis, expose concentration, or support a decision.

## Reconcile

Components must reconcile to the aggregate unless exclusions, overlaps, weighting, or grain differences are explicit. Watch for Simpson's paradox, denominator shifts, composition effects, and double counting.

## Re-aggregate meaningfully

After finding detailed patterns, regroup them into a smaller decision model. Good synthesis preserves decisive differences without reproducing every segment.

## Diagnostic questions

- Is the aggregate hiding opposing segment movements?
- Is a segment effect actually caused by changing composition?
- Are rates weighted by the correct exposure or denominator?
- Which components explain most of the total change?
- Is the chosen hierarchy exhaustive and non-overlapping enough for the decision?
- What detail can be collapsed without losing causal or strategic meaning?

## Output

State the aggregate signal, the two or three disaggregated drivers that matter, any reconciliation gap, and the decision implication.
