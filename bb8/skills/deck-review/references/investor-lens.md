# Investor Lens

What a reader is checking at each point in a pitch deck, from published YC and
a16z guidance. Source keys in [brackets] are listed at the end. These are
principles, not benchmarks. Anything with a number in it is dated, so treat it
as context, not as a current market standard.

## The core model

- The reader **looks for a reason to stop** until something earns the next
  meeting. VCs see many deals and invest in very few [Ralston-Seed]. At Demo
  Day investors mostly decide who they don't need to talk to [Ralston-DD].
- **Most investors decide in the first few minutes** whether a company seems
  like a winner [PG-convince]. If they don't get a slide's point right away,
  they check email [Hale].
- **The reader's mode flips.** Investors who like the founders "reach for
  ideas" ("yes, and you could also do x"). Those who don't ask "but what about
  y?" [PG-convince]. Every company has reasons to invest and reasons not to;
  the reader's mode decides which ones they cite.
- **The deck has to survive being retold.** A partner retells it to
  colleagues [PG-convince]. Readers take away only one or two things [SR-2min; Hale].
- **What they are really judging:** formidable founders, a promising market,
  and usually some evidence of success so far [PG-convince].

## The reading sequence

The order of slides can vary ("no fixed format or order" [Ralston-Seed;
Ralston-DD]). The reader's questions, though, come up in roughly this order.

### 1. Comprehension: cover and first slides
- *Silent question:* "What is this, for whom, and can I picture someone using it?"
- Start with the name and what it does, in plain words. Seibel's example:
  "Socialcam is a mobile app that makes it easy to take videos and share them
  with friends and family" [Seibel]. Ralston-DD: "We deliver groceries to
  customers in their homes", not "a next-generation, AI-based resolver of
  grocery needs".
- Don't hold back what the product does "until half your pitch is done" [Ralston-DD].
- *Thoughts that end the read:* "I still don't know what they do." "Platform…
  solution… leveraging AI…" Jargon and marketing-speak read as incompetence
  [PG-convince; Seibel]. Buzzwords used loosely weaken the pitch [Casado].
- *Test:* Seibel's email test. Send a two-sentence explanation to a smart
  friend; if they ask clarifying questions, revise [Seibel].

### 2. Market and why now
- *Silent question:* "Is this big, and why is it possible now?"
- "Founders think of startups as ideas, but investors think of them as markets"
  [PG-convince].
- Build the market bottom-up (number of customers × price). Every source that
  covers sizing prefers it [Seibel; Ralston-DD; Blackappl; a16z-16more]. A
  top-down "1% of $X trillion" invites "why that share?" [a16z-16more] and
  "silly market size numbers without clear justification" are on YC's list of
  things not to do [Ralston-Seed].
- **Why now:** the trend you will benefit from [PG-convince; SR-pitch].
- **Insight:** something you know that others don't, stated specifically and
  without complicated language [Seibel]. Andrew Chen calls this an "earned
  secret" [SR-Chen]. Ralston-DD's questions: why hasn't this been done before,
  and why is it hard?
- *Thoughts that end the read:* "Made-up number." "Why isn't an incumbent doing this?"

### 3. Proof: product and traction
- *Silent question:* "Is this real, and how fast are they moving?"
- **Progress relative to time:** "how much progress have you made relative to
  the time you've been working on something?" [Harris-pod; Seibel]. Product and
  customers count for more than fundraising or partnership deals [Seibel].
- **Size first, then growth.** Investors check size (revenue, GMV, bookings)
  first, then growth [a16z-16 #16].
- **Product slide:** does it show what the product does *today*, or the
  roadmap? [SR-Chen]. A product they can see is usually not enough evidence on
  its own [Ralston-Seed].
- **Projections:** do they only restate last year, or explain how the business
  is about to inflect? [SR-Chen]. Detailed financials at seed are on YC's list
  of things not to do [Ralston-Seed].
- **Honesty:** traction must be presented as "non-fiction". Exaggerations are
  "fatal errors" [Ralston-DD]. Investors "are far better at detecting bullshit
  than you are at producing it" [PG-convince]. Over-claiming rarely looks good
  [Casado]. See [the metric tripwires](metric-tripwires.md).
- Testimonial slides "feel good but [have] almost no impact" [Ralston-DD].
- *Thoughts that end the read:* "Four years in, and only an alpha" [Harris-pod].
  "This chart always goes up; what's it hiding?"

### 4. Team
- *Silent question:* "Why these people, for this problem?"
- "Investors are investing in you and your team above all" [Ralston-DD];
  "Investors invest in teams not slides" [Hale].
- Include only relevant, notable facts: how many founders, whether there is a
  technical cofounder, whether everyone is full-time, how long they've worked
  together, and domain credentials in regulated or technical markets [Seibel;
  Blackappl]. Skip GPAs and irrelevant employers [Seibel].
- Keep it terse but brag with specifics, such as "zero to $X million in revenue"
  (the "dinner party jerk" test). "We don't need to know you are childhood
  friends" [SR-Chen].
- Formidable means "justifiably confident". Acted swagger is easy to spot [PG-convince].
- **Swap test:** if someone else gave this pitch, would it still make sense?
  Ideally it would not [SR-2min].

### 5. The ask
- *Silent question:* "What do they want, and what does it buy?"
- Ask explicitly: "If you want me to invest, ask" [Seibel].
- The amount must be "tied to a believable plan" with 12–18 months of milestones
  [Ralston-Seed]. Harris wants an 18–24 month plan with "a continuous line
  from one thing to another", and no leap from today to a huge future without
  a bridge [Harris-pod].
- Use of funds should name which channels, segments, and features, not just
  budget categories [a16z-cmd].
- *Thoughts that end the read:* no ask; raising 3–5 years of runway
  (over-capitalisation) [Harris-pod]; opening with a price expectation [a16z-cmd].

### 6. Close and memory
- End with the 3–4 points the reader should remember. Ralston-DD calls them
  "vertebrae"; Blackappl closes on the three most powerful facts.
- *Exit test:* write the retell sentence and the 3–4 points. If you can't,
  the story has no spine.

## Stage calibration

| Stage | The bet is on [SR-Chen] | What the deck should lean on |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-seed | Team | founder–market fit, an earned insight, early pull, speed of building |
| Seed | Product | a working product, usage or retention, progress relative to time |
| Series A | Traction | growth with honest metrics: monthly growth (CMGR), cohorts, paid CAC |
| Series B and later | Revenue, then unit economics | burn multiple, Rule of 40, CAC payback [a16z-cmd] |

PG: at Demo Day a company only needs to look like "a promising experiment";
by Series A it needs to show the experiment worked [PG-convince]. Pre-traction
decks can succeed, but then the story, the founders, and the vision carry the
weight [Ralston-DD].

## Sent vs presented

| Rule | Presented (live) | Sent (read alone) |
|---|---|---|
| Length | ~5–7 ideas; one idea per slide [Hale] | ~10–12 sections plus appendix; still one main point per slide [Ralston-Seed] |
| Words | "more than 7 words on a slide is likely too many" [Ralston-DD] | full-sentence headlines; must make sense without narration |
| Screenshots / video | screenshots are "almost always illegible"; video seldom works [Hale; Ralston-DD] | "Graphics, charts, screenshots are more powerful than lots of words" [Ralston-Seed] |
| Charts | state the conclusion in words above the chart [Hale] | same, plus labelled axes and units |
| Main risk | the audience doesn't know whether to watch the slides or the speaker [Ralston-DD] | a deck that only works with the founder narrating |

Harris advises not attaching a big deck unless asked, and offering to pitch
first [Harris-pod]. So a sent deck is often the reader's **first and only**
impression, which makes it stricter, not looser.

## Contradictions, resolved

- **Fixed order vs any order:** order is flexible, but what the company does
  comes first, and the strongest fact must not be buried ("Don't bury the
  lead", Justin Kan via [Ralston-DD]).
- **High-level metrics vs rigorous definitions:** show little detail, but
  define honestly what you do show [Harris-pod vs a16z-16]. Not a real conflict.
- **Growth vs efficiency:** Chen warns founders against focusing on profit over
  growth [SR-Chen]; the 2023 a16z piece says "growth at all costs" has ended
  [a16z-cmd]. This is a real disagreement that depends on the market and stage.
  Ask about the round context; don't pick a side.
- **Simulating the investor vs Horowitz's warning** against pitching to "what
  does Ben want" [SR-Ben]: the simulation tests clarity and credibility of the
  company's own story ("the story of the company is also the strategy of the
  company"). It must not reshape the story to imagined taste.
