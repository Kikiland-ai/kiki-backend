# Deep Research

Produce an evidence-backed answer, not a decorated search summary.

Read [the evidence protocol](references/evidence-protocol.md) for source selection, contradiction handling, and confidence labels.

## Workflow

1. Clarify the decision, audience, scope, time horizon, and acceptable uncertainty.
2. Decompose the question into answerable research branches and identify decisive unknowns.
3. Form a search plan that prioritizes primary and authoritative sources.
4. Gather evidence broadly enough to detect disagreement, then deepen the highest-value branches.
5. Distinguish facts, source claims, calculations, interpretations, and hypotheses.
6. Compare dates, definitions, populations, incentives, and methodologies before calling sources contradictory.
7. Triangulate material claims and identify what remains unsupported.
8. Synthesize around the user's decision, implications, tradeoffs, and next evidence—not source-by-source summaries.

## Output

Lead with the answer and confidence. Include the research scope, decisive evidence, contradictions, implications, uncertainties, and practical next steps. Cite claims close to the text they support.

## Boundaries

- Never invent citations, quotations, dates, or source access.
- Current claims require current verification.
- Do not treat search-result snippets as reviewed sources.
- Do not equate repeated secondary reporting with independent confirmation.
- Respect source and copyright limits; paraphrase unless a short quotation is necessary.
- Label inference explicitly.
