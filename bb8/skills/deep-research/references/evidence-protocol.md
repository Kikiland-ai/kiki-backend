# Evidence Protocol

## Source order

Prefer sources closest to the underlying event, rule, dataset, or decision:

1. Primary records, official documentation, original datasets, filings, standards, and research papers.
2. High-quality independent analysis with transparent methodology.
3. Reputable reporting that identifies its evidence.
4. Expert commentary and practitioner accounts.
5. Aggregators or community discussion for leads, not final authority.

Source authority is claim-specific. A company is authoritative about what it announced, not necessarily whether the announcement will work.

## Evidence ledger

For material claims, track:

| Claim | Source | Date | Evidence type | Supports or challenges | Confidence | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Do not show the full ledger unless useful, but use it internally to prevent citation drift.

## Contradictions

Before declaring conflict, check:

- terminology and units;
- event date versus publication date;
- population, geography, segment, and time horizon;
- measured result versus forecast;
- gross versus net, nominal versus real, and correlation versus causation;
- source incentives and methodology.

Classify remaining conflict as real, apparent, or unresolved. State the evidence that would resolve it.

## Confidence

- **High:** direct, current, independently corroborated evidence.
- **Moderate:** credible evidence with limited triangulation or material caveats.
- **Low:** sparse, indirect, old, or contested evidence.
- **Unknown:** evidence is unavailable or insufficient.

Confidence belongs to individual claims and conclusions, not the document as a whole.

## Stopping rule

Stop when additional research is unlikely to change the decision, reduce a material uncertainty, or reveal an important alternative. Do not confuse exhaustiveness with rigor.
