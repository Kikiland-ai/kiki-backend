# Minto Communication

Make the logic easy to grasp without flattening meaningful nuance.

Read [the pyramid protocol](references/pyramid-protocol.md) before restructuring a substantial argument.

## Workflow

1. Identify the audience, decision, desired response, and evidence already established.
2. Write the governing thought as the most useful answer the evidence supports.
3. Determine the question naturally raised by that thought.
4. Group supporting points by a consistent logical relationship.
5. Check vertical logic: every lower level answers the question raised above it.
6. Check horizontal logic: sibling points are comparable, ordered, and collectively sufficient for the claim.
7. Choose an introduction that establishes situation, complication, question, and answer when useful.
8. Remove repetition, throat-clearing, and unsupported certainty.

## Output

Provide the governing thought, argument map, and audience-ready narrative. Preserve caveats that could change the decision.

## Boundaries

- Do not force three points or perfect MECE when the evidence has a different natural shape.
- Do not turn assumptions into facts to make the pyramid look complete.
- Do not bury the answer behind chronology unless sequence is itself the argument.
- Do not confuse a topic label with a complete assertion.
