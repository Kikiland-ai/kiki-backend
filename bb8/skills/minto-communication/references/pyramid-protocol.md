# Pyramid Protocol

## Governing thought

Express one complete assertion that answers the audience's controlling question. It should contain the subject, judgment, and significance—not merely a topic.

## Vertical logic

Each statement should raise a natural question answered by the level below:

- Why is this true?
- How should this be done?
- What does this consist of?
- What follows from this?

If the lower level answers a different question, restructure the argument.

## Horizontal logic

Sibling points should share one relationship:

- deductive steps;
- inductive reasons;
- components of a whole;
- stages in a process;
- mutually useful options or criteria.

State the grouping principle. Order points by causal importance, chronology, structure, priority, or another audience-relevant logic.

## Introduction

Use Situation–Complication–Question–Answer when it improves orientation:

- **Situation:** relevant shared context.
- **Complication:** what changed, failed, or created tension.
- **Question:** the decision or issue now raised.
- **Answer:** the governing thought.

Do not force SCQA when the audience already knows the context or needs an immediate answer.

## Integrity checks

- Every claim is supported by the level below.
- Sibling points do not mix reasons, actions, and examples.
- Caveats that change the decision remain visible.
- Evidence is not duplicated under several branches.
- The structure reflects the reasoning rather than decorating it.
