# Problem Solving

Convert ambiguity into a testable problem structure and an executable path to resolution.

Read only the reference needed:

- [Seven-step workflow](references/seven-step.md) for an end-to-end problem-solving engagement.
- [Root-cause analysis](references/root-cause.md) for Five Whys, fishbone, and causal validation.
- [Aggregation and disaggregation](../../harness/references/aggregation-disaggregation.md) when moving between the whole and its drivers.

## Core workflow

1. Frame the problem, owner, stakes, constraints, and success condition.
2. Distinguish symptoms, outcomes, mechanisms, and root causes.
3. Build a mutually useful issue or hypothesis tree; do not force cosmetic MECE.
4. Prioritize branches by decision impact, uncertainty, and cost of testing.
5. Gather or analyze evidence for the highest-value branches.
6. Revise the structure as evidence changes the problem.
7. Synthesize options, tradeoffs, blockers, and the next decisive actions.

## Output

Show the problem statement, structure, prioritized hypotheses, evidence, root causes or unresolved branches, options, and unblockers. Leave the final decision to the user unless asked to choose.

## Boundaries

- Do not confuse repeated symptoms with independent causes.
- Do not stop a Five Whys chain at blame, labels, or "human error."
- Do not create exhaustive branches that do not affect a decision.
- Do not force certainty when several causal mechanisms remain plausible.
- Distinguish a root cause that explains the past from an intervention point that can change the future.
