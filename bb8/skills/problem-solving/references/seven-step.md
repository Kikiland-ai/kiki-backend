# AI-First Seven-Step Problem Solving

## 1. Define the problem

Write the gap between current and desired state, who owns it, who experiences it, the time horizon, constraints, and a measurable success condition. Challenge solution-shaped problem statements.

## 2. Structure the problem

Build an issue tree or system map. Separate outcomes, mechanisms, constraints, and interventions. Use MECE as a diagnostic aid, not a cosmetic requirement.

## 3. Form hypotheses

Turn branches into falsifiable explanations or solution hypotheses. Record what evidence would support or reject each one.

## 4. Prioritize

Rank branches by potential impact, uncertainty, reversibility, dependency, cost of evidence, and information value. Investigate decisive branches first.

## 5. Analyze

Use AI to accelerate search, synthesis, coding, scenario generation, and pattern detection. Require source traceability, reproducible calculations, and human judgment for ambiguous definitions, ethics, and high-stakes choices.

## 6. Synthesize

Update the model from evidence. State what is known, inferred, contested, or unknown. Explain the few findings that change the decision.

## 7. Act and learn

Define Now, Next, and Later; owners or capabilities; leading indicators; validation checkpoints; exit criteria; and the feedback loop that updates the original hypothesis tree.

## AI-first does not mean AI-only

Use AI where it reduces search or transformation cost. Keep human ownership of intent, values, authorization, and irreversible decisions. Do not automate uncertainty out of sight.
