# Findings Rubric

A strong finding contains:

1. **Pattern:** what repeatedly or consequentially occurred.
2. **Context:** when, where, and for whom it occurs.
3. **Mechanism:** why it appears to happen.
4. **Significance:** what outcome, decision, or experience it affects.
5. **Evidence:** traceable transcript moments and source diversity.
6. **Counterevidence:** exceptions, disagreement, and alternate interpretations.
7. **Confidence:** strength and limitations of the evidence.

## Distinctions

- **Signal:** one potentially meaningful observation.
- **Theme:** a cluster of related signals.
- **Finding:** an evidence-backed explanation with significance.
- **Insight:** a finding that changes how the problem or opportunity is understood.
- **Opportunity:** a valuable outcome space suggested by findings.
- **Recommendation:** a proposed response requiring its own tradeoff analysis.

Do not promote a signal directly to an insight because it is vivid. Do not use counts alone when sessions differ in length, prompts, or participant composition.

## Confidence labels

- **Strong:** repeated across relevant sources with a clear mechanism and limited counterevidence.
- **Moderate:** plausible pattern with useful evidence but important gaps.
- **Emerging:** early signal worth further research.
- **Contested:** material evidence points in different directions.
