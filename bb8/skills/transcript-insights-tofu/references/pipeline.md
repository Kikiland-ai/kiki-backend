# Transcript Analysis Pipeline

## Stage 1: Intake

Record source, date, session type, participants or roles, consent or sensitivity constraints, research question, facilitator context, and transcript-quality limitations.

## Stage 2: Normalize

Correct obvious formatting or speaker-label problems without silently rewriting meaning. Preserve raw source references, timestamps, and uncertain passages.

## Stage 3: Context reconstruction

Build a compact model of goals, terminology, actors, constraints, chronology, and the situation that prompted the conversation.

## Stage 4: Atomic extraction

Capture evidence units with source location:

- observation;
- direct statement or short quote;
- decision;
- action and owner;
- question;
- assumption;
- need or desired outcome;
- workaround;
- pain or friction;
- tension or contradiction;
- emotional or confidence signal when supported.

## Stage 5: Clustering

Group by mechanism or meaning, not shared words alone. Preserve participant, segment, and session differences. Track disconfirming examples.

## Stage 6: Finding development

Turn a cluster into a finding only after articulating the pattern, context, mechanism, significance, evidence, counterevidence, and confidence.

## Stage 7: Opportunity formation

Translate findings into opportunity areas or questions before solutions. Connect each opportunity to affected users, desired outcomes, evidence, and uncertainty.

## Stage 8: Handoff

Provide the evidence map, decisions, actions, findings, opportunities, unresolved questions, and recommended next research. Make cold pickup possible without rereading every transcript.
