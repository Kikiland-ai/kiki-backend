# Launch-week streaming playbook (excerpt)

> **Illustrative.** An invented creator agency's onboarding playbook, written as input for KiKi's tribal knowledge builder. It is not a real agency's document. GTA 6 is referred to by name only. The rules below are the agency's rules of thumb, not statistics about any platform.

Applies to: newly signed creators, launch week of GTA 6 · Owner: talent development (illustrative) · Version 1 · Section IDs (LW-x.y) are the source tags KiKi's lessons cite.

## 1. Why launch week is different

Everyone streams the same game. Many viewers arrive while browsing the game's category and hop between streams. They decide in the first minutes whether to stay, and again every time your stream goes slow. Regulars forgive a slow segment; browsers don't.

## 2. Stream structure: the 3-hour template

| Block | Length | What it's for |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open | 0–5 min | Start with the hook: what's happening today and why it's worth staying. "Starting soon" under 5 minutes |
| Plan | 5–10 min | Say today's goals out loud; mods pin them in chat |
| High-intensity block | 20–30 min | A mission, a heist, a challenge |
| Low-intensity block | 10 min at most | Grind, travel, menus, loading: only with a chat mechanic running (LW-5.2) |
| Repeat | | Alternate high and low. Never two low blocks in a row |
| Collab or raid | Mid-stream | Bring a new audience in near your peak hour |
| Wind-down | Last 20–30 min | Q&A, tomorrow's plan, raid out |

## 3. Hooks

- Renew the hook every 10–15 minutes: a stake, a question, a countdown, a choice for chat.
- Say the next segment's goal before you start it: "Next 10 minutes: cash for the car, and chat picks the colour."
- **LW-3.1** No more than 60 seconds of silence on stream. If you need to concentrate, narrate what you're doing.

## 4. Chat engagement

- In low-intensity blocks, read chat out loud at least once a minute. Name regulars; welcome first-time chatters.
- **LW-4.1** Run a poll or a prediction during every grind or loading stretch.
- Mods pin the run-of-show and the current goal.
- **Chat rate is the early warning.** When chat goes quiet, viewers start leaving a few minutes later.

## 5. Segment planning

- **LW-5.1** Plan every stream as a run-of-show with segment names and target lengths. Send it to your manager by 18:00.
- **LW-5.2** Grind segments: 10 minutes at most, a goal said out loud, a chat mechanic running.
- Keep a filler segment ready for queues, crashes and loading: Q&A, clip review, a chat challenge.

## 6. Reading analytics (15 minutes after every stream)

- Put your segment bands on the CCV graph. Find the biggest drop and the biggest gain, and the segment each sits in.
- Compare chat rate with CCV around each change: chat usually moves first.
- **LW-6.1 Segment retention** = CCV at the segment's last minute ÷ CCV at its first minute. Below 0.8, the segment lost people.
- Count follows per segment, not just per stream.
- Watch the VOD for 2 minutes either side of the biggest drop.
- **LW-6.2** Change one thing per stream and log it: the change, the effect you expect, the measure, the result.

## 7. Raids and collabs

- Launch week: one collab every two streams, with an agency creator of similar size.
- Incoming raid: stop, thank the raiding channel by name, give a 60-second recap of what's going on, then carry on.
- Outgoing raid: raid an agency partner who is live, and tell chat why you're sending them there.

## 8. Language strategy

- Stream in the language your community chats in. For most of our Hindi-belt creators that's Hinglish. Keep game and platform terms in English: CCV, raid, VOD.
- Titles and tags in English and Hindi, so the stream is found in both.
- When a raid arrives from a community that speaks another language, welcome them in it for the first minute.

## 9. IP and brand safety

- Refer to the game by name. Don't put publisher logos or key art in thumbnails, overlays or panels unless the publisher's published content policy allows it; when in doubt, ask your manager.
- No leaks or spoilers in titles.
