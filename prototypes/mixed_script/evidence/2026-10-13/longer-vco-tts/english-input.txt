A voltage-controlled oscillator (VCO) changes its output frequency according to its control voltage. In direct frequency modulation (FM), the message signal drives the VCO's control input. In an ideal linear model, f_i(t) = f_c + K_v m(t), so the carrier's instantaneous frequency varies while its amplitude remains constant. Here f_c is the carrier frequency, K_v is the VCO sensitivity in Hz/V, and m(t) is the message voltage. For example, in this ideal model, let f_c = 1000 Hz, K_v = 100 Hz/V, and m(t) = 0.5 V. Substituting these values gives f_i(t) = 1050 Hz, an increase of 50 Hz above f_c. If m(t) = -0.5 V instead, the same ideal model gives f_i(t) = 950 Hz, a decrease of 50 Hz below f_c. These examples describe the assumed ideal relationship, not a guarantee that every real VCO has a perfectly linear response or constant amplitude over its entire operating range.
